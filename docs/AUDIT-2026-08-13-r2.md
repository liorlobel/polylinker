## Verdict

The numeric core is now the best-verified part of this tree by a wide margin and
should stop being audited. Twenty-one oracles at zero disagreements, 210,721
cross-frontend assertions, 91,884 exhaustive rotations, 4,640 Biopython
restriction comparisons and a byte-for-byte TypeScript/Rust geometry diff
produced **one** surviving finding inside `crates/`, and it is the least severe
of the fourteen. Thirteen of fourteen survivors are in presentation, glue, or
non-Rust code.

One finding is serious and it is a release blocker, not an audit item.
`bins/pl-gui/src/clone.rs:1670` advances the product cursor by the wrong strand's
length, so **533 of the 1,535 two-enzyme religations I could test put a carried
feature on bases that are not its own** — while all 54 single-enzyme controls are
clean. A double digest is the most common cloning operation there is. The
mechanism was named as a "weaker claim" by round 1 and left unfixed; the round-1
fix then wrote an eight-line comment asserting that the case cannot arise. It
arises in a third of the enzyme table.

Everything else is a count printed from the wrong field, a hedge lost at a
surface boundary, a check that compares zero items, or a figure that draws
something the data did not say. No other finding moves a base.

### 1. `bins/pl-gui/src/clone.rs:1670` — wrong-answer — HIGH — **REGRESSION FROM A ROUND-1 FIX**

**This is the one category the brief asks to flag prominently, and this is the
only finding in it.**

`build` computes `let flen = frags[*idx].watson.len() as u64;` (clone.rs:1635) and
uses it for two different jobs: the arc length in the **parent** — correct, that
is what `locate` measured — and the advance of the **product** cursor,
`at += flen` (clone.rs:1670). But `ligate::join` builds the product as
`watson: format!("{}{}", a.watson, b.watson)` (crates/pl-clone/src/ligate.rs:236)
and `ligate::flip` sets `watson: d.crick.clone()` (ligate.rs:201). A fragment laid
in flipped therefore contributes `crick`, of length `c`, not `watson`, of length
`w`. Whenever `c != w` the cursor drifts by `w - c` for every fragment laid after
it, and `place`'s only guard — `lo < 1 || hi > slot.product`, clone.rs:1806 —
catches nothing, because the drifted coordinate is comfortably inside the
construct. That is precisely the failure mode this file's own doc at :1560 names.

`c != w` is not exotic. It is the ordinary shape of a **double digest**: EcoRI and
PstI both leave four-base overhangs, but of opposite polarity, so `w - c = ±8`.

**Why this is a regression rather than a rediscovery.** Round 1's finding #2 fixed
the mirror and explicitly left this half open, in these words: *"A secondary,
weaker claim: `at += flen` … is also wrong whenever a flipped fragment has
`crick.len() != watson.len()` … I could not construct a concrete digest in which
such a fragment both flips and seals, so treat that half as unproven."* At
`f0e4a6f` the code carried a four-line comment. The fix replaced it with an
eight-line comment (clone.rs:1620-1634) that converts "I could not construct one"
into three affirmative claims, **all three false**:

- *"every fragment of a single-enzyme digest"* has `w == c` — false for every
  terminal fragment of a linear parent. Measured with the shipped binary:
  `pl cut-adapter` on a one-site linear molecule gives `{w 21, c 25, ovhg 0}` and
  `{w 25, c 21, ovhg -4}`.
- *"every fragment of a digest by enzymes that share an overhang width"* — false;
  EcoRI and PstI share the width and differ in polarity, giving `w - c = ±8`.
- *"no digest is known that both produces such a fragment and seals it flipped"* —
  false for a third of the enzyme table, below.

A fix that closes half a finding and writes into the source a claim that the other
half is unreachable is worse than leaving it open. House rule 2 was breached by
the repair.

**Failure scenario.** A user opens a plasmid with two EcoRI and two PstI sites,
opens Cut and religate, ticks both (clone.rs:466 renders an independent checkbox
per cutting enzyme), and clicks Open on one of the offered circular products
(clone.rs:710). The construct opens, the map draws the feature, `Save ▸ GenBank`
writes `misc_feature complement(…)`, and the Copy-record text says the construct
was assembled correctly. Nothing on screen says the coordinate is eight bases out.

**Executed, against the real Rust at HEAD.** Household pairs, 264 bp circle, two
sites of each enzyme, one 30 bp feature per gap:

```
EcoRI+PstI   : 4 fragments, 6 circular products,  4 misplaced
HindIII+SacI : 4 fragments, 6 circular products,  4 misplaced
XbaI+SphI    : 4 fragments, 6 circular products,  4 misplaced
EcoRI+SacI   : 4 fragments, 6 circular products,  4 misplaced
BamHI+KpnI   : 5 fragments, 24 circular products, 16 misplaced
EcoRI+BamHI  : 4 fragments, 6 circular products,  0 misplaced
```

and one misplacement in full, so the shape is visible:

```
gene3 [(0,false),(1,false),(3,true),(2,true)] at 216..245
  got  ACGAAGCTGAACTTACCTTACATGAAAACG
  want ATCACACCCGTTTTCATGTAAGGTAAGTTC
  rc   GAACTTACCTTACATGAAAACGGGTGTGAT      <- sits at 224..253, exactly 8 bases later
```

Eight is EcoRI's four-base 5' overhang plus PstI's four-base 3' overhang. Over the
whole table:

```
unambiguous enzymes: 58
SINGLE-ENZYME CONTROL (4 sites): clean 54, misplacing 0, skipped 4
ENZYME PAIRS (2+2 sites):        tested 1535, clean 1002, MISPLACING 533, skipped 118
```

**The one-line fix, applied and measured.** Replacing only the cursor term:

```rust
at += if *flipped { frags[*idx].crick.len() as u64 } else { flen };
```

```
SINGLE-ENZYME CONTROL: clean 54, misplacing 0     (unchanged)
ENZYME PAIRS:          clean 1535, MISPLACING 0
```

533 → 0, controls unchanged.

**Why the tests miss it, and the sharpest fact in this document.** `clone.rs` has
16 `ticked(&[...])` calls. **Exactly one passes two enzymes: `["EcoRI","BamHI"]`
at clone.rs:2164** — both four-base 5' cutters, so `w == c` for every fragment.
That is the one household pair in my sweep that comes back clean. And
`reference/python/tests/xcheck_clone.py` cuts with exactly one enzyme per case
(`enz = getattr(Restriction, case["enzyme"])`), so the mixed-polarity shape is
outside the pydna oracle too. Applying the fix above leaves **all 20 runnable
shipped tests green** — the file's own suite cannot distinguish the defect from
its repair.

**Fix.** The one line above, plus a test on a circle with two EcoRI and two PstI
sites that reads each carried feature back off the product's own bases with the
`bases()` oracle the file already has (clone.rs:1845, whose own doc says a number
compared against a number cannot see this). **Delete** the comment at
clone.rs:1620-1634 rather than editing it; all three of its claims are false.
The sweep harness reproduces in fifteen minutes with `rustc` and no `target` lock.

### 2. `bins/pl-gui/src/clone.rs:1811` — wrong-answer — MEDIUM

`place` mirrors each segment (`m - b, m - a`, :1799-1801) and `.collect()`s them
in the **parent's** order (:1811); the caller assigns `moved.segments = segments`
and flips only `strand` (:1653-1665). On a flipped fragment `a < b` maps to
`m-b < m-a`, so a two-exon feature comes back **descending**. Nothing re-sorts —
a repo-wide grep for segment reversal outside the parser finds nothing, and
`pl.exe convert --to genbank --stdout` returns both orders byte-unchanged.

The repository states the convention this breaks, in three places:
`crates/pl-fileio/src/genbank.rs:611` — "`Feature::segments` is stored in join
order and a Reverse feature is read back to front" — enforced by `segs.reverse()`
at :646 and validated at `bins/pl-gui/src/aa.rs:878-886` against pKoV SacB's
stored `/translation`. A descending list is therefore read back-to-front into
ascending splice order: **the exons are swapped**.

**Failure scenario.** A two-exon CDS (a real intron, or any origin-crossing
feature, which GenBank stores as a two-part join) travels on a fragment laid in
inverted. `Save ▸ GenBank` writes `complement(join(33..36,27..30))`. Biopython
1.87 on that file extracts `CCATCTTG` where the gene's spliced sequence is
`CTTGCCAT`; the ascending spelling extracts `CTTGCCAT` and agrees. A translated
protein that is not the one in the tube.

**Coverage.** None. All 13 `segments.push` calls in `clone.rs`'s test module build
single-segment features. The defect is symmetric — a Reverse parent flipped to
Forward is stored wrong the same way.

**Fix.** `if slot.flipped { out.reverse(); }` before returning. Pin it with a
fixture feature given two exons, taking the inverted product, and asserting the
spliced bases (concatenating `rc()` of the parts in reverse) equal the parent's.

*Not a regression:* `place` is new code from the round-1 fix, but the pre-fix
inline `.map(...).collect()` at `f0e4a6f:1512-1526` had the same order behaviour.
Carried forward, not introduced.

### 3. `bins/pl/src/main.rs:2845` — wrong-answer — MEDIUM

`query::run` caps *retention* at `MAX_RETAINED_HITS = 1_000_000` across the whole
result set while keeping the true per-record count in `Match::hits_total` and the
shortfall in `Results::dropped_hits`. Every consumer prints `m.hits.len()`:
main.rs:2845 (text row), :2792 (`"hits":` in `--json`),
`bins/pl-gui/src/main.rs:7434` (Library tab). `hits_total` and `dropped_hits` are
read by **nothing** outside `pl-index`'s own unit tests. Once the budget is spent
the cap is 0 and later records come back with an **empty** `hits` vec while
`found` is still true — so the record is reported as matching, with zero hits.

`crates/pl-index/src/query.rs:230` says this must not happen, in as many words:
"A caller that prints `hits.len()` as 'the number of sites' is printing a display
artefact as a fact about a plasmid."

**Executed.** 20 files × 2 Mbase = 40 Mbase of random ACGT — a size `pl index`
itself blesses ("a motif search will take roughly 119 ms") — motif `ATG`:

```
"path": "g16.fa", "hits": 1663
"path": "g17.fa", "hits": 0
"path": "g18.fa", "hits": 0
"path": "g19.fa", "hits": 0
"total_hits": 1248241
```

Indexing `g19.fa` alone gives `"hits": 62757`; an independent Biopython count of
the same file, circularised, both strands, gives fwd 31,391 + rev 31,366 =
**62,757**. Three records holding ~62,000 start codons each are reported to a
script as containing zero, and a record's reported count is a function of what
else happens to sit in the directory. On a poly-A corpus the text rows sum to
exactly 1,000,000 under a footer reading `2000040 hit(s) in total` — the
contradiction is on one screen.

**Coverage.** The crate's own test (`query.rs:731`) pins the behaviour perfectly
including `dropped_hits == half`. No test anywhere drives a result set past the
bound through a binary; `bins/pl/tests/cli.rs` has real `pl find` tests and none
reaches it.

**Fix.** Print `m.hits_total` at main.rs:2792, :2845 and pl-gui main.rs:7434, and
when `results.dropped_hits > 0` add one line saying the coordinates shown are a
prefix — the CLI already has that pattern at main.rs:2865 for `--limit`.

### 4. `bins/pl/src/main.rs:2851` — wrong-answer — MEDIUM

The result row prints length, topology, `m.row.path` and the hits. `Row::record`
and `Row::name` are both indexed (`crates/pl-index/src/lib.rs:123`, :148) and both
reach `--json` (main.rs:2787-2788); neither reaches the text output. A file
holding several records produces rows that are byte-identical up to the hit list,
and the hit coordinates are **record-local** (`scan.rs:222-248` offsets into the
shared store by `row.seq_off`). This defeats the stated reason for indexing per
record: `pl-index/src/lib.rs:117` — "Records, not files: a 124-record `.gbk` is
124 rows."

**Executed**, on the repository's own `tests/library-fixture/multi.gb`:

```
$ pl find <corpus> --motif A --index-at <idx>
        20 bp    circular  multi.gb  10 hits at 1+, 4-, 5+, 8-, ...
        20 bp    circular  multi.gb  4 hits at 2+, 3+, 4-, 5-
$ ... --json
  {"path": "multi.gb", "record": 0, "name": "m1", ..., "hits": 10},
  {"path": "multi.gb", "record": 1, "name": "m2", ..., "hits": 4}
```

`--text AmpR`, which matches against `Row::name`, prints the file twice and never
shows the name it matched.

**The codebase already applies the opposite rule everywhere else**, which is what
makes this a defect rather than a preference: `pl find-motif` prints
"2 records in this file; only the first was searched"; `pl info` prints
"records 2 in this file; showing the first"; `convert` and `export` refuse
multi-record files outright; the GUI renders `format!("{} #{}", path, record + 1)`
at pl-gui main.rs:7421; and main.rs:18 states the principle. `pl find`'s text mode
is the one surface that omits it while printing record-local coordinates.

**Fix.** Append ` #N` when `record > 0` and include `m.row.name`, as the GUI does.
Pin it over the shipped `multi.gb`, asserting the two rows differ.

## Recommended next pass

**Do not run a fourth sweep of `crates/`.** Four rounds, exhaustive fuzzing, 21
oracles, a byte-for-byte TypeScript diff and 210,721 cross-frontend assertions
have yielded one LOW finding there. The Rust core is the best-verified part of
this tree. Further sweeping it is theatre.

The remaining defects are all in code that nobody has executed, has no oracle, or
is not Rust. In priority order:

**R3-P0 — Fix `clone.rs:1670` now; this is a release blocker, not an audit item.**
533 of 1,535 enzyme pairs misplace a carried feature, all 54 single-enzyme
controls are clean, the one-line fix takes the count to zero, and the file's own
suite is blind to both states. Do not wait for round 3 to confirm it — it is
confirmed against the real Rust at HEAD. Delete the comment at clone.rs:1620-1634
in the same commit; all three of its claims are false. Add the EcoRI+PstI test,
which is ten lines in the existing harness. Fix finding 2 in the same pass — it is
three lines away and the same feature-placement path.

**R3-P1 — Give `polylinker.exe` a headless entry point, then drive it.** Nothing
else unblocks the largest hole in this tree's testing, and that hole is where the
survivors are. Concretely: add `--version` (the commit is already compiled in via
`PL_COMMIT`), and add an `--exec <script>` or egui test-harness entry so an
auditor can open a file, run a clone plan, and dump panel state as JSON without a
window.

**R3-P2 — Read `bins/pl/tests/cli.rs` and `crates/pl-draw/src/tests.rs` by hand,
name against fixture.** 114 tests, 5,917 lines, standing behind the two artefacts
every round-2 unit used as ground truth. If a `cli.rs` test compares zero items,
"pl.exe agrees with pydna" rests on an unexamined foundation. Scanners cannot find
this shape — round 1 established that its five scanners are blind to it, and it
produced four of its nineteen findings.

**R3-P3 — Audit `prototype/dna-reader.template.html` and put `prototype/` inside
the gate.** 1,257 lines, called "Usable today" in the README, a documented
silent-data-loss history, outside `ci.ps1` entirely, unopened in four audits.
Build it with `tools/build-web.ps1`, open it, and put one molecule through it.
Cheapest high-severity real estate left.

**R3-P4 — `bins/pl-gui/src/sfnt.rs` and the ~2,500 lines of `main.rs` tests that
consume it (16046-18570).** The remaining half of round 1's P3, and the two
halves are the same hole. An independent fontTools check run for this document
says the property currently holds — under harfrust's 14 default-on features, the
mono face (`IBMPlexMono-Regular`, the only face in `FontFamily::Monospace`) has
**zero ASCII-reachable GSUB/GPOS rules** — so this is now about whether the guard
can fail, not about the font. Note that Plex Sans carries an ASCII `f+i` ligature
and 72 ASCII kern pairs, and Inter-SemiBold carries `calt` rules turning `->` into
an arrow glyph; both are Proportional/Heading only, so cosmetic today.

**R3-P5 — Buy a corpus, or write the disclaimer.** Fifty real Addgene `.dna` files
and twenty real `.ab1` traces cost a download. Until then seven numeric claims in
the shipped prose are unverifiable and have been for two rounds, and house rule 2
arguably already requires marking them in the source as "measured on a corpus not
in this repository, on `<date>`" rather than leaving them as bare assertions.

**One process fix worth more than any of the above.** Three of this round's eleven
units abandoned a testable surface with a false "could not check" —
`dump_spline.exe` was on disk, `pl-mcp.exe` was provably at HEAD, `PL_COMMIT` was
embedded in `polylinker.exe` — and the round's only HIGH finding was left as a
Python transcription while another unit in the same round was compiling GUI
modules with `rustc`. Round 1's lesson was *run things*. Round 2's lesson is
**before writing "could not", spend five minutes trying, and read what the other
units did.** If there is a round 3, give it a shared five-line preflight —
`git diff --stat <lastbuild>..HEAD`, a byte search for `PL_COMMIT`,
`ls target/release/examples/`, `ls ~/.cargo/bin/rustc` — and require every
`could_not_check` to cite a command that failed.
